Here is a question that has confused physicists, philosophers, and children in roughly equal measure: why does a mirror flip left and right, but not up and down?

Stand in front of a mirror. Raise your right hand. The reflection raises its left hand. But your head stays on top. Your feet stay on the bottom. The mirror seems to have reversed one axis and left the other two alone. Why would a flat piece of glass discriminate between horizontal and vertical?

The answer is that it does not. A mirror does not flip left and right. It never has. What a mirror actually does is flip front and back.

Point at the mirror. Your finger aims forward, toward the glass. The reflection's finger points backward, toward you. That is the only reversal the mirror performs. It takes the axis perpendicular to its surface and inverts it. Front becomes back. Everything else stays exactly where it is. Your right hand is still on the right side of the reflection. Your head is still on top.

So where does the left-right illusion come from? From you. When you see a figure facing you in the mirror, your brain instinctively compares it to another person standing in front of you. To face you, a real person would have turned around, rotating 180 degrees on a vertical axis. That rotation swaps left and right. The mirror did not do that rotation. You mentally imposed it. The "reversal" is not in the physics. It is in the cognitive act of comparison.

This is surprisingly hard to internalize, even once you understand it. Richard Feynman used to delight in watching people tie themselves into knots over this question. The philosopher Jonathan Bennett wrote an entire paper in 1970 arguing that the question itself is malformed. Martin Gardner devoted a chapter of "The Ambidextrous Universe" to it. The consensus among all of them: the mirror is innocent. The confusion lives entirely in how human spatial reasoning handles reflections versus rotations.

What I find remarkable is how perfectly this demonstrates the gap between perception and reality. The mirror is doing one simple, honest thing: reversing the depth axis. No selectivity, no preference, no trick. But because humans carry a deeply ingrained model of what "facing someone" looks like, they perceive an asymmetry that is not there. The illusion is so strong that most people will argue with you about it. They will insist the mirror is flipping left and right because they can see it happening. And in a sense they can. They are just watching their own cognition, not the mirror.

There is something poetic about an object whose only function is to show you yourself, and whose most famous property is an illusion created by the viewer. The mirror does not lie. It does not even distort. It performs one inversion, the simplest possible transformation of three-dimensional space, and humans project a phantom asymmetry onto the result because their brains cannot help modeling the reflection as a person who turned around.

Every mirror you have ever looked into was telling you the truth. You were the one flipping things.
